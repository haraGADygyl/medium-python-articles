I added `tests/test_plan.py` with 18 tests covering `split_days` and `describe_plan`. All 18 pass, the full suite passes (60 tests), and ruff is clean on the new file. Nothing under `src/` changed, and I didn't commit.

The tests are based on the README and docstrings:

- **`split_days`**
  - Days are split when the next leg would exceed the limit, and a day may exactly reach it.
  - A leg longer than the limit becomes a day by itself.
  - The default limit is 7 hours.
  - Fitness scales leg times.
  - Hours are rounded to 0.01.
  - A zero or negative limit, or zero fitness, raises `ValueError`.
  - Days cover the whole track and their total matches `summarise(...).hours` within rounding.
- **`describe_plan`**
  - One line per day in the documented format.
  - Durations round to the nearest minute, including a carry into the next hour (`1.999` → `2h 00m`).
  - An empty plan gives an empty list.

To get exact expected values, most tests use tracks with no horizontal distance, where a leg's Naismith time is exactly `climb / 600` hours.

Two behaviours the tests pin down that the docs don't state explicitly:

- Consecutive days share a boundary point: the last point of one day is the first point of the next.
- The last day ends at `len(points) - 1`.

Two things I noticed but did not test:

- The limit is compared against unrounded hours, but the reported hours are rounded to 0.01. With a limit like 6.996, a day of 6.996 h is reported as 7.0, which is slightly over the limit.
- `split_days` on a `Track` with fewer than two points doesn't raise. `parse_track` prevents this, but a directly built `Track` can hit it.
