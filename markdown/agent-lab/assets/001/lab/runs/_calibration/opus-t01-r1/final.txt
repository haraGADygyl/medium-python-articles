I wrote `tests/test_elevation.py` with 34 tests. The full suite passes (72 tests) and `ruff check` is clean. Nothing under `src/` was changed.

What's covered:

- **`gain_and_loss`**
  - Simple climbs and descents, plus empty, single-value and flat inputs.
  - The README's two promises: a metre of jitter either way adds nothing, and a 300 m climb (or descent) in 1 m steps counts as 300 m.
  - Threshold edges: a change of exactly the threshold counts, and one just below it doesn't.
  - Changes are measured from the last *counted* elevation, not the previous point.
  - Custom and zero thresholds.
  - A negative threshold raises `ValueError`, even when the list is empty.
  - Totals are rounded to 0.1 m and returned as floats, and the 3 m default is checked.
- **`highest_and_lowest`**: ordinary, flat and below-sea-level tracks.
- **`steepest_grade`**
  - The README example (18 m over 100 m gives `18.0`).
  - Downhill legs count, with a positive result.
  - The steepest single leg wins, rather than the overall slope of the track.
  - Results are rounded to 0.1, and legs running east–west and north–south both work.
  - Repeated points (zero-length legs) are skipped: no division by zero, and their elevation change is ignored.

To get legs of an exact length, the test points sit on the equator and their spacing is worked out from the radius constant in `trailhead.geo`. That keeps the expected grades exact rather than approximate.

All tests passed on the first run, so I found no differences between the code and what the README and docstrings describe.
